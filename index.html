Four kinds of energy you need

Energy is a number (a scalar), measured in joules. It has no direction, so you just add the amounts.

EnergyFormulaUse it whenRemember
KineticK = ½mv²Anything is movingNever negative. Twice as fast means 4 times the K.
Gravitational, near EarthUg = mgyObjects near the ground (balls, ramps, roller coasters)y is the height above a zero you choose. Only the change in height matters.
Spring (elastic)Us = ½kx²A spring is stretched or squeezedx is measured from the relaxed length. Twice the squeeze means 4 times the energy.
Gravitational, far awayUG = −Gm₁m₂/rSatellites, planets, escapeZero at infinity, so it is always negative. Farther away means less negative.

The one big rule

Total mechanical energy is E = K + U.

If nothing from outside the system does work, E stays the same: K₁ + U₁ = K₂ + U₂.

If something does work on the system, or friction turns energy into heat: K₁ + U₁ + Woutside = K₂ + U₂ + thermal.

Every energy problem in four steps.

1. Choose the system (what is inside, what is outside).
2. Pick a start moment and an end moment.
3. List every energy at the start and at the end. Drawing bars helps.
4. Write start + work in = end, and solve.

In falling problems the mass cancels: mgh = ½mv², so v = √(2gh).

Roller coaster: K and U trade places

Going down, potential energy turns into kinetic energy. Going up, it turns back. With no friction, the total stays exactly the same.

Watch the three bars while the ball rolls. With no friction, green (U) plus orange (K) always add up to the starting energy.

Speed depends only on how far it has dropped, not on the shape of the track: v = √(2g × drop).

Turn on friction and a red bar (thermal energy) grows. The ball can no longer reach its starting height, and it finally stops at the bottom.

Trap: “It is 15 m above the ground, so use h = 15.” No. Use how far it has dropped from where it started.

Try it. Release from 4 m with no friction. Where is the ball fastest? Does it slow down on the small hill in the middle? Does it ever go higher than 4 m? Now change the mass: does the speed change?

Springs: squeeze, launch, climb

A squeezed spring stores energy Us = ½kx². Let go, and it becomes kinetic energy, then gravitational energy as the block climbs.

k is how stiff the spring is (N/m). x is how far it is squeezed from its relaxed length.

The energy chain on a frictionless track:

½kx² = ½mv² = mgh

So the launch speed is v = x√(k/m), and the block climbs to h = kx²/(2mg).

The squares matter. Squeeze twice as far: 4 times the energy, 2 times the speed, 4 times the height.

Try it. Set x = 0.2 m, then 0.4 m. How many times higher does the block go? Now double the mass: what happens to the speed and the height?

Orbits: energy below zero

Far from Earth, we use UG = −GMm/r. It is zero at infinity, so every closer position is negative.

Think of it as a hole: the satellite is “stuck” in Earth’s energy well. Zero is the top of the hole (infinitely far away).

For a circular orbit:

K = GMm/(2r), U = −GMm/r, and E = K + U = −GMm/(2r).

A higher orbit is slower (smaller K), but U is less negative, and the total E is higher. Moving up costs energy.

Escape speed: give the satellite just enough kinetic energy that the total is zero. Then it slows down forever and stops only at infinity. vesc = √(2GM/r).

Try it. Drag the orbit farther out. Which bars shrink? Then press “Escape speed”: what is the total energy?
